## Taking a Break
 I once had a particularly heinous set of data from a lab so I went to my instructor for help. Completely baffled by my results, he wasn’t sure if there was anything I could do because “you can’t make a silk purse out of a sow’s ear”. How was I supposed to complete my lab? I went from being discouraged to devastated. It was the dead of winter but I needed to get away from the university so I just started walking. I trudged through the snow until my cheeks were red and my eyelashes froze. At first, I was trying to sort through the problem but there is something about froze eyelashes that give you real perspective on life. Feeling greatly refreshed, I returned to work and eventually figured out several scenarios which could have caused the bad data (which is almost as good as good data).

 ## Happy Accidents
 Many remarkable scientific discoveries were made completely by accident and as it has been said “Chance favors the prepared mind.”  Students need to be taught that mistakes are only a bad thing when we fail to learn from them.  Students need to understand that when something does not work as predicted, one should not toss out the results and try again but rather determine why the unexpected occurred.
